Upper-left lockup — three revisions

The site is a patient-first reference, not a clinic, not a journal, not a health app. The upper-left is the “who is speaking” moment. Right now it speaks in an app-icon dialect (rounded teal tile) that fights that job — especially on the share card, where the same tile already sits giant on the right.

Objective filter: every lockup has to say “calm educational reference for a person in pain,” own painmedicine.com against the OUP journal Pain Medicine, and keep the settling-signal story readable at 32px. If it looks like Apple Health, a clinic portal, or a journal masthead, it fails.

C · Divergent

Rest-line masthead

The name is the signal. Pain, then a settling rule, then rest under .com. Most on-metaphor. Weakest as a system: stretching the path into an underline turns it back into a chart — the OG mistake we just walked away from — and it doesn’t survive 32px chrome or a favicon.

Steal from this: the idea that the rest line is the brand, not the spike. Don’t steal the stretch.

Recommendation

Ship B in two surfaces, keep the tile only where an icon is required:

  1. Header / footer — free-floating teal signal + “Pain Medicine” + .com. No rounded square. That’s the publication.
  2. OG / Twitter upper-left — wordmark + .com only. Delete the 52px mini-tile. The 320px tile on the right already is the image; repeating it upper-left makes the card look like a template.
  3. Favicon, Apple, PWA, social avatar — keep today’s teal tile. Those surfaces need a contained icon. Don’t force the editorial lockup into a 16px tab.

Optional later, not in B: a one-line kicker under the wordmark (“Patient guide” / “The specialty, explained”) if journal-collision shows up in the wild. Don’t add it until the name-plus-.com isn’t doing the job.
