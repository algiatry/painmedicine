# Content ideas spun from Pain Medicine News

> **Purpose.** Pain Medicine News (painmedicinenews.com) is a clinician trade
> publication: its readers are anesthesiologists and pain physicians, its
> stories are study write-ups, society statements, and practice news, and
> most of it sits behind a free-registration wall. None of that is our
> reader. But every clinician headline is a patient question in disguise,
> and the primary studies underneath are exactly the sources the house
> standard asks for. This file takes the strongest ideas from their
> current front page (pulled 2026-10-04) and spins each into a
> patient-first page for this site.

**The spin rule.** Keep the evidence, flip the audience. A PMN story asks
"what should I prescribe or bill?"; our page asks "what does this mean for
the person sitting in the waiting room?" Every idea below follows the
pattern: clinician headline → patient search query → page in the house
voice (describe, never prescribe; cited to the primary study, not to PMN).

Never cite Pain Medicine News itself as a reference. It is a secondary
source and paywalled. Cite the study it reports on, verified below by
PubMed ID.

## Priority ideas

Ranked by search demand × fit with the existing hubs × how much of the
primary evidence is already in hand. Effort: **S** = update an existing
page, **M** = new spoke page, **L** = new spoke page plus figure(s).

### 1. Do opioids work for acute pain? — the Sydney overview

- **PMN story:** "New Review Finds Opioids Often Ineffective for Acute Pain"
  (Policy & Legislation, Aug 2026).
- **Primary source (verified):** *Efficacy and Harms of Opioid Analgesics
  for Acute Pain: Overview of Systematic Reviews and Meta-analyses.*
  Drugs 2026;86:533-550. PMID 41739420.
- **Patient query:** "do opioids work for acute pain", "are opioids
  stronger than ibuprofen", "will I need opioids after surgery / for a
  broken bone / for back pain".
- **Spin:** A plain-English page on what the largest overview of the
  evidence actually found: small, short-term benefit for some acute
  conditions, no clear benefit for others (notably back and neck pain),
  and harms that are not small. Frame as literacy, not policy: this is
  why a clinician may reach for an NSAID first, and why "they only gave me
  ibuprofen" is often the evidence working, not the system failing you.
- **Where:** `/treatments/opioids-for-acute-pain` (new, **M**), or fold a
  section plus FAQ into `/treatments/opioid-stewardship` (**S**). Prefer
  the new page: the stewardship page is about long-term use, and this
  query is acute.
- **House-rule hazards:** opioid content → SAMHSA helpline inline; no
  dosing; nothing that reads as "refuse opioids after surgery". Pair with
  `/conditions/persistent-postsurgical-pain` and the medications page.

### 2. Switching from high-dose opioids to buprenorphine

- **PMN story:** "Buprenorphine Study Results in Small Reduction in Pain,
  Opioid Use" (Primary Care, Aug 2025).
- **Primary source (verified):** *Buprenorphine, Pain, and Opioid Use in
  Patients Taking High-Dose Long-Term Opioids: A Randomized Clinical
  Trial.* JAMA Intern Med 2025. PMID 39960730. (Being offered the switch
  cut full-agonist opioid use from roughly 157 to 94 MME; pain fell about
  0.7 points on the Brief Pain Inventory; results were similar whether or
  not patients actually switched.)
- **Patient query:** "buprenorphine for chronic pain", "switching from
  oxycodone to buprenorphine", "is buprenorphine only for addiction".
- **Spin:** The stigma question first ("it's the addiction drug, isn't it?"),
  then what buprenorphine is (a partial agonist with a ceiling on breathing
  suppression), then what the trial honestly showed: a large drop in
  full-agonist exposure, a modest change in pain, and the striking finding
  that pain did not get worse. Routes the decision to the prescriber.
- **Where:** `/treatments/buprenorphine-for-pain` (new, **M**). Link both
  ways with `/treatments/opioid-stewardship`.
- **House-rule hazards:** opioid content → SAMHSA inline; no taper
  schedules; no "how to obtain".

### 3. Spinal cord stimulation: what the big studies say now

- **PMN story:** "Large Study Points to Benefit of Spinal Cord Stimulation
  for Chronic Pain" (Multimedia, Sep 2026, paywalled; the specific study is
  not identifiable from the preview).
- **Primary sources (verified, nearby):** *Burst vs Tonic Spinal Cord
  Stimulation for Chronic Neuropathic Pain: A Systematic Review and
  Meta-Analysis of Multidimensional Outcomes.* Neuromodulation 2026.
  PMID 42742508. *Longitudinal changes in pain catastrophizing and
  multidimensional health outcomes after passive-recharge burst spinal
  cord stimulation: cohort with two-year follow-up.* Acta Neurochir 2026.
  PMID 42747509. Confirm the PMN study once the article is read with a
  free account.
- **Patient query:** "does spinal cord stimulation work", "spinal cord
  stimulator success rate", "spinal cord stimulator long term results".
- **Spin:** We already describe SCS on `/treatments/interventional-procedures`
  and `/future-of-pain-medicine/neuromodulation`. The gap is a
  results-focused FAQ pair: "What percentage of people get relief?" and
  "Does it wear off?", answered from the meta-analysis with honest
  numbers, plus the trial-stimulation "test drive" point we already make.
- **Where:** two FAQs plus a short section on the interventional page
  (**S**). A standalone `/treatments/spinal-cord-stimulation` spoke is a
  reasonable later step if GSC shows the query cluster.

### 4. Steroid injection or physiotherapy for shoulder pain?

- **PMN story:** "Advantages of Steroids Versus Physiotherapy for Shoulder
  Pain" (Primary Care, Aug 2026): a one-year pragmatic trial; injections
  gave faster but shorter-lived relief than a 12-session physiotherapy
  course, and the evidence is not strong enough to say when or for whom.
- **Primary source:** not yet identified; the PubMed hit for 2026 shoulder
  corticosteroid-plus-physiotherapy (PMID 42346753) is a different design.
  Find the pragmatic trial PMN reports before drafting, or anchor on the
  established Cochrane review of corticosteroid injections for shoulder
  pain.
- **Patient query:** "cortisone shot or physical therapy for shoulder",
  "how long does a cortisone shot last shoulder".
- **Spin:** A new condition page, `/conditions/shoulder-pain` (**L**), built
  on the "fast but short vs slow but durable" trade-off, which is also the
  lesson of `/treatments/comparing-your-options`. Shoulder pain is the
  third most common musculoskeletal complaint and the site has no page
  for it: this is the highest-demand gap on the list.

### 5. At-home ketamine: what the anesthesiologists are warning about

- **PMN story:** "ASA Cautions Against At-Home Ketamine Delivery, Calls for
  Stronger Safeguards" (Practice Management, Sep 2026).
- **Primary source:** the ASA statement itself (asahq.org newsroom), plus
  FDA's 2023 compounded-ketamine safety alert and the existing evidence
  reviews for ketamine in chronic pain.
- **Patient query:** "ketamine for chronic pain", "ketamine infusion for
  pain does it work", "at home ketamine safe".
- **Spin:** The site has no ketamine content at all, and the query volume
  is real. A page in the Kratom mould: what ketamine is, what the evidence
  for pain shows (short-term relief after infusions for some neuropathic
  and CRPS pain, weak long-term data), why monitored infusion differs from
  mailed lozenges, and what the ASA is asking for. Describe, never
  prescribe.
- **Where:** `/treatments/ketamine` (new, **M**).
- **House-rule hazards:** controlled substance → no "how to obtain"; no
  dosing; link `/find-help`.

### 6. Music, cartoons, and the science of distraction

- **PMN story:** "Music, Cartoons Help Pediatric Patients Alleviate Pain
  Following Surgery" (Clinical, Oct 2026).
- **Primary source (from the PMN preview):** Pain Management Nursing,
  Sep 22 2026, doi 10.1016/j.pmn.2026.09.020 (114 children aged 3–6;
  venipuncture distress fell with music or cartoons; oxygen saturation
  unchanged). Verify the DOI resolves before citing.
- **Patient query:** "how to distract a child during a shot", "does music
  help with pain", "distraction for pain".
- **Spin:** Not a pediatrics page (out of scope) but an *Understanding
  Pain* explainer: why attention changes pain. It is the gate-control
  story from `/understanding-pain/how-pain-works` with a lived example,
  and it sits naturally next to the placebo and nocebo pages.
- **Where:** `/understanding-pain/attention-and-distraction` (new, **M**),
  or a section plus FAQ on `how-pain-works` (**S**).

### 7. Nature, green space, and pain

- **PMN story:** "Prescribing Nature: The Emerging Role of Nature-Based
  Therapies in Pain Medicine" (Complementary, Aug 2026).
- **Primary sources (verified):** *Effect of nature-based sounds on pain
  and anxiety during dressing changes in hospitalized burn patients: RCT.*
  Burns 2025. PMID 41066932. *Immersive nature-based virtual reality for
  chronic pain.* Curr Opin Support Palliat Care 2025. PMID 40679918.
- **Patient query:** "nature therapy chronic pain", "does being outside
  help pain", "virtual reality for pain".
- **Spin:** Honest and small: the evidence is early, mostly short-term,
  mostly about distress rather than tissue. Frame it as a low-risk
  adjunct, exactly as the acupuncture FAQ on `comparing-your-options`
  does. Good candidate for the *Future of Pain Medicine* hub, alongside
  `mind-and-brain`.
- **Where:** a section plus FAQ on `/future-of-pain-medicine/mind-and-brain`
  (**S**).

### 8. The new non-opioid acute pain drugs, patient edition

- **PMN story:** monograph "New and Emerging Developments in Nonopioid
  Analgesics for the Management of Acute Pain" (Viscusi, de Leon-Casasola,
  Sep 2026).
- **Spin:** We already have the strongest patient page on this topic
  (`/treatments/medications-for-pain`, suzetrigine section, and the
  pipeline tracker). The spin is a *check*, not a page: read the monograph
  with a free account and confirm the pipeline tracker lists every agent
  it names (next-generation NaV1.8 blockers, cebranopadol, the
  long-acting local anesthetics). Add any that are missing.
- **Where:** `src/lib/pipeline.ts` (**S**).

### 9. Why opioid overdose deaths are falling

- **PMN story:** "CDC Reports Decreased Opioid Overdose Deaths" (Policy,
  Jun 2026).
- **Primary source:** CDC provisional overdose counts (VSRR) and the
  accompanying CDC release.
- **Spin:** A one-paragraph update plus FAQ on `/treatments/opioid-stewardship`:
  what the numbers show, what is credited (naloxone access, buprenorphine
  access after the X-waiver ended, changes in the supply), and why that
  matters to someone on a long-term prescription. Pairs with idea 2.
- **Where:** `/treatments/opioid-stewardship` (**S**).

### 10. Groin pain: the hernia that isn't

- **PMN story:** "Core Conversations: Managing Groin Pain" (podcast,
  Sep 2026).
- **Patient query:** "groin pain not hernia", "groin pain causes", "pain in
  groin when walking".
- **Spin:** Groin pain appears on the site only in passing. It is a
  high-volume, poorly served query with real diagnostic breadth (hip joint,
  adductor, nerve entrapment, sports hernia, referred spinal pain). The
  page would be the differential, told the way `/conditions/low-back-pain`
  tells it: what can hurt, what the exam looks for, and the red flags.
- **Where:** `/conditions/groin-pain` (new, **L**; needs a PelvisPosterior-
  style anatomy figure). Sources to gather: a recent review of chronic
  groin pain in Br J Sports Med or similar, plus NIAMS/NHS patient pages.

## Not worth spinning

Clinician-only stories with no patient question behind them:
anesthesia coding audits, NORA scheduling, the pain fellowship debate,
mentorship columns, AI in pediatric anesthesia, nonsterile ultrasound gel
infections, the brachial plexus block hypersensitivity study, and the ASHP
drug-spending projection. The cannabis-and-lung-cancer story is real
patient news but belongs on a cannabis page the site does not yet have;
park it until one is planned.

## Suggested order

1. Idea 1 (opioids for acute pain) and idea 3 (SCS FAQs): the evidence is
   in hand and both strengthen pages that already rank.
2. Idea 4 (shoulder pain) and idea 10 (groin pain): the two biggest
   condition gaps on the site.
3. Idea 5 (ketamine) and idea 2 (buprenorphine): new treatment spokes with
   clear query demand and verified sources.
4. Ideas 6, 7, 8, 9: small updates to existing pages, batchable in one
   sprint.

Every page follows CONTENT-TEMPLATE.md: ~60-word answer, 3–6 FAQs, three
or more primary references, `status: "sourced"` until a council reviewer
signs off. Run `npm run lint` before shipping; the content lint will catch
a missing SAMHSA line on the opioid pages.
