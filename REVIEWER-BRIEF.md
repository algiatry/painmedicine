## The role: Medical Reviewer

**What you would do**
- Review clinical pages (condition guides, treatment overviews) for medical
  accuracy and currency before they publish.
- Confirm each page's claims are supported by its cited sources and consistent
  with current guidelines.
- Flag anything inaccurate, out of date, missing, or poorly framed.
- Approve ("sign off") pages you're comfortable with. Your name, credentials,
  and the review date then appear on the page as *"Medically reviewed by
  [Name], [Credentials] on [Date]."*
- Optionally: re-review on the update cadence (future-science pages quarterly;
  condition/treatment pages annually or on major guideline changes).

**What you would NOT do**
- Write pages from scratch (unless you want to — that can be a separate paid
  scope).
- Provide medical advice to individuals or take on any patient-care duty.
- Assume liability for patient outcomes. You are attesting that *educational
  content* is accurate — the site is not a provider of care and says so on every
  page.

**Format:** fully remote, asynchronous, batched. A well-drafted, pre-cited page
typically takes **~30–60 minutes** to review.

## Who we're looking for (credentials)

Publishable credentials, in rough priority for pain content:

- **MD or DO**, board-certified in **Pain Medicine** — the strongest fit. (Six
  ABMS boards now certify it — anesthesiology, PM&R, psychiatry & neurology,
  emergency medicine, family medicine, radiology — all via the same
  ACGME-fellowship + shared-exam pathway.)
- **MD/DO** in a closely related specialty (anesthesiology, PM&R, neurology,
  rheumatology, physiatry) for most condition pages.
- **PharmD** — ideal for the medication/pharmacology pages (analgesics,
  adjuvants, opioid stewardship, the new non-opioid classes).
- **DPT** (physical therapy) or **PhD** (pain neuroscience) — for the physical,
  behavioral, and mechanism pages.

Requirements: active, unrestricted license in good standing; comfortable being
named publicly with credentials; no undisclosed conflicts of interest.

A single reviewer can cover the launch set. Two (e.g., a pain physician + a
PharmD for meds) is ideal but not required to start.

## Compensation (you set the terms)

Pick whatever fits — these are common market shapes, not fixed offers:

- **Per-page honorarium** — simplest. Medical review of a pre-drafted, pre-cited
  page commonly runs **~$100–$300/page** depending on length and specialty.
- **Hourly** — expert medical review/consulting commonly runs **~$150–$400/hr**.
- **Monthly retainer** — a fixed sum for up to N pages/month + re-reviews.
- **Founding Medical Advisor** — title + a modest equity/advisory grant in lieu
  of (or alongside) cash, if the site becomes commercial. Attractive to
  early-career physicians building a public profile.

Whatever you choose, put it in writing (see the agreement note below).

## How the process works (the editorial gate)

1. **We draft & pre-cite.** Each page is written to the house standard with a
   references list of primary sources (see `CONTENT-INTAKE.md`).
2. **You review.** You get the draft (Google Doc / PDF / Markdown — your
   preference) with its sources. You check accuracy, flag issues, request edits.
3. **We revise** until you're comfortable.
4. **You sign off.** You complete a short attestation (name, credentials, date,
   statement of accuracy, consent to be named). See the form in
   `CONTENT-INTAKE.md`.
5. **We publish** with your byline and the review date. You can ask to have your
   name removed at any time.

## Boundaries & a light agreement (protects you both)

Before engaging, put a one-page letter of agreement in place covering:

- **Scope:** review of educational content only; no patient care, no
  individualized advice.
- **Attestation:** reviewer confirms accuracy to the best of their professional
  knowledge as of the review date — not a guarantee of outcomes.
- **Naming:** consent to publish name + credentials + date; right to withdraw
  the name (and thus the "reviewed" status) at any time.
- **Independence & disclosure:** disclose any conflicts (industry ties, etc.).
- **Payment terms.**
- **Indemnification / liability:** the site carries prominent disclaimers on
  every page; clarify the reviewer is not liable for reader actions.

> This brief is practical guidance, not legal advice. Have an attorney review any
> binding agreement, and let the reviewer confirm the arrangement sits fine with
> their own malpractice/professional-liability coverage. (Ask if you'd like a
> plain first-draft agreement to take to counsel.)

## Where to find candidates

- **Professional societies** — post to or ask for member referrals: American
  Society of Regional Anesthesia and Pain Medicine (ASRA), American Academy of
  Pain Medicine (AAPM), American Academy of Physical Medicine & Rehabilitation
  (AAPM&R). Many have job boards or member directories.
- **LinkedIn** — search *"board certified pain medicine"* combined with *"medical
  writer,"* *"medical reviewer,"* or *"medical advisor."* Many physicians
  actively seek this work.
- **Academic pain centers & fellowship programs** — email program coordinators;
  fellows finishing training and junior attendings are often keen.
- **Physician-side-gig & medical-writing communities** — e.g., physician
  freelancing groups, medical-writer associations (AMWA), and reputable
  medical-review services.
- **Telehealth clinician networks** — many clinicians there do async work.
- **Semi-retired physicians** — experienced, credible, and often have time.
- **Your own network** — a warm introduction beats a cold post every time.

Start with 3–5 warm or targeted outreaches rather than a broad post; you only
need one good reviewer to unlock the launch set.

## Before you engage (verification checklist)

- [ ] **Verify the license** on the relevant **state medical board** site
      (active, unrestricted, no disciplinary actions).
- [ ] **Verify board certification** via the ABMS lookup at
      **abms.org/verify-certification** (Certification Matters) or the relevant
      specialty board.
- [ ] **Confirm identity** (a brief video call).
- [ ] **Get written consent** to publish name + credentials + review date.
- [ ] **Sign the one-page agreement.**
- [ ] Record exactly how the credential line should read, e.g.
      *"MD, board-certified in Pain Medicine (ABA)"* — this becomes the byline.

ready to move into review.
